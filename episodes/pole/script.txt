Mila has a whole empty square. She's about to hit the only pole.
Grandpa jogs behind, holding the seat. She wobbles.
Then he lets go. She's riding alone!
Then she sees the pole.
The bike curves toward it, like a magnet.
Bonk. Pigeons everywhere.
All that space. Wait. Why the pole?
Your hands tend to follow your eyes. In a driving simulator, people told to look off to one side drifted that way, while trying to drive straight.
Studies of drivers on twisty roads show their eyes reach each bend a second or two early.
Motorcycle trainers call it target fixation. Stare at danger, and you tend to drift toward it.
So look at the gap, not the pole. Like a doorway: you watch the opening, not the frame.
Mila tries again. Eyes on the gap. She sails through.
Grandpa wants a turn. He stares straight at the pole.
What's the pole you can't stop staring at?
