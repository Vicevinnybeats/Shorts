Here's the secret. In many lifts, that button does nothing at all.
In the United States, a disability law from 1990 says lift doors must stay open long enough for someone on crutches or in a wheelchair to get in. So in many lifts, the close button was simply switched off. Firefighters can still use it, with a special key.
And it's not just lifts. In 2004, New York City admitted that more than two thousand five hundred of its crosswalk buttons did nothing.
So why keep them? Because pressing a button feels like control. Psychologists call it the illusion of control.
