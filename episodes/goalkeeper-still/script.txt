Leo saved the biggest penalty of his life. Everyone thought he froze.
Final minute. Last kick. The striker runs up, and every goalkeeper on Earth does the same thing. They dive.
Researchers studied two hundred and eighty six penalty kicks in top leagues. Keepers dived left or right about ninety four times out of a hundred.
Leo doesn't. He stays right in the middle.
The ball comes straight at him. Saved.
The crowd laughs. He didn't even move!
But the study found exactly this. Given where strikers really aim, staying in the middle was the best bet.
So why does almost nobody do it?
Because a goal after a dive feels like bad luck. A goal while you stood still feels like your fault.
Psychologists call it action bias. We would rather fail doing something than fail doing nothing.
There is a catch, though. If every keeper copied Leo, strikers would simply aim for the middle.
Would you have had the nerve to stand still?
