Ben just won the auction. And he just lost money.
School charity night. Parents, paddles, and one giant jar of coins.
Nobody knows what's inside. Everyone guesses.
Ten dollars. Fifteen. Twenty. Ben keeps his paddle up.
Sold, for twenty five dollars!
Ben counts the coins. Eighteen dollars. His smile drops.
Wait. He won. So why does winning feel like losing?
It's like a crowd guessing the sweets in a jar. Some guess low, some guess high.
But who wins an auction? The highest guess. Often the biggest overestimate.
In a classroom test, coin jars worth eight dollars sold for about ten. The average guess was about five.
Oil engineers named it, after firms seemed to overpay for offshore oil fields. The winner's curse.
The fix? Bid below your own guess. More bidders? Step back further.
Next year, Ben bids low. An excited parent outbids him. Ben just smiles.
So, would you still raise your paddle?
