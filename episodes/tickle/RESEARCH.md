# Research: You Can't Tickle Yourself. Here's Why

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Self-produced touch is rated less ticklish than the same touch produced externally | Verified (Blakemore, Wolpert & Frith 1998, Nature Neuroscience 1(7):635-640) | https://web.math.princeton.edu/~sswang/autism/blakemore_frith98_NN.pdf ; https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/2870 |
| The brain predicts the sensory result of its own movement (a copy of the motor command, "forecast") and turns the response down when it matches | Verified: the authors' forward-model explanation; script simplifies it as "a copy to itself" | same |
| Brain scans (fMRI) point to the cerebellum as the predictor | Verified as the authors' proposal: cerebellum predicts sensory consequences and supplies the attenuation signal; less somatosensory activity for self-touch. Script says scans "point to" it, not "prove" | https://web.math.princeton.edu/~sswang/autism/blakemore_frith98_NN.pdf ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6975290 |
| Someone else's touch can't be predicted the same way, so it tickles | Verified (externally produced stimulus rated more tickly; attenuation tied to prediction) | Blakemore et al. 1998/1999 |
| 1999 robot study: people moved a robot arm with the left hand, which moved a second robot that stroked their right palm (soft foam) | Verified (Blakemore, Frith & Wolpert 1999, J. Cognitive Neuroscience 11(5):551-559). Script says "tickled their own palm through a robot" | https://Gwern.net/doc/psychology/neuroscience/1999-blakemore.pdf ; https://columbia.demo.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/spatio-temporal-prediction-modulates-the-perception-of-self-produ-3 |
| Delays of 0, 100, 200, 300 ms; tickliness rose with delay up to 200 ms ("a fifth of a second"); trajectory rotation also raised it | Verified (rise reported up to 200 ms; it then levels off, so the script says a fifth of a second made it "tickle more", not "tickle like someone else") | same; https://www.rdasia.com/?p=1958 |
| Why the brain does it: muting predicted self-made sensations makes external signals stand out (same principle as the dampened response to your own voice) | Verified as the mainstream interpretation; script says "makes everyone else's stand out" | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3965634 ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4155919 |
