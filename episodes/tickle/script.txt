Theo can tickle anyone into tears. Except himself.
Saturday night. Theo attacks the sofa. Nia shrieks. Dad begs for mercy.
Then he tries his own ribs. Nothing.
A feather. Harder. Still nothing.
Wait. Why?
When you move, your brain sends the order to your hand, and a copy to itself.
That copy is a forecast. The real touch matches it, so your brain turns it down.
Fingers from someone else come with no forecast. Every poke is a surprise.
Brain scans point to the cerebellum, at the back of your head, as the forecaster.
In nineteen ninety nine, people tickled their own palm through a robot.
Add a delay of a fifth of a second, and the forecast misses. It tickled more.
Why bother? Muting your own touch makes everyone else's stand out.
So Theo builds a machine. String, a feather, and a delay crank.
It works. A tiny giggle. Nia rolls her eyes.
Would you build one, or let your sister do it?
