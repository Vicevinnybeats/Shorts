Last slice of pizza. One coin. Zoe knows something her brother doesn't.
Friday night. Zoe and Leo reach for the final slice at the same time. Leo pulls out a coin. "You call it."
Zoe watches his thumb. The coin is sitting heads up.
"Heads," she says.
Leo grins. Fifty-fifty, right?
Not quite.
Scientists filmed forty-eight people flipping coins from dozens of currencies. Three hundred and fifty thousand flips.
The coin landed on the same side it started on about fifty-one percent of the time.
Why? A flipped coin doesn't just spin. It wobbles. So it spends a little more of its flight with the starting side facing up.
And heads or tails on its own? No difference at all. Only the starting side matters.
It's a tiny edge. Some flippers showed none. But over a thousand bets, you would come out ahead.
The coin spins. Leo catches it.
Heads. Zoe takes the slice.
Leo looks at the coin. "Best of three?"
Would you call the side facing up?
