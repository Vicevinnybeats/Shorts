Lucas ordered two pizzas. Amira ordered one. She got more pizza.
Friday night. The pizzeria is packed.
Lucas grins. Two twelve inch pizzas, please. Amira points. One eighteen inch.
The boxes land. Lucas pats his stack. Two beats one.
Then the lids open. Hers looks enormous.
Wait. Two pizzas, less pizza? Why?
Menus sell pizza by width. One line, edge to edge.
But pizza is a surface. Twice as wide means four times the pizza. Like tiling a floor: double both sides, and you need four times the tiles.
So one eighteen inch pizza: about two hundred fifty four square inches. Two twelve inch pizzas: about two hundred twenty six.
Studies suggest we judge size along one line, then don't adjust enough for the rest.
And per bite, the large is often cheaper too.
Next Friday, Lucas orders one large. And a second one, just in case.
So, would you still order two?
