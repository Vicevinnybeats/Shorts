What if you could hold your breath like a GTA 6 character? Swim to the bottom, look around, take your time. Let's test that.
Test one. Holding your breath on land. In the game, the air bar barely moves.
In real life, most adults can hold their breath for about thirty to ninety seconds.
Trained freedivers practice for years to reach several minutes. The record without extra oxygen is over eleven minutes.
Test two. Cold water. In the game, the sea feels like a warm bath.
In real life, falling into cold water makes you gasp in the first minute, whether you want to or not.
That's why rescuers teach the one, ten, one rule. One minute to control your breathing. About ten minutes of useful movement. Around an hour before hypothermia takes over.
Test three. Diving games. In the game, you can stay down as long as you like.
In real life, pushing a breath hold underwater can make you black out without warning.
That's why safety experts say: never hold your breath underwater alone, and never ignore the urge to breathe.
In the game, your air bar refills at the surface. In real life, a buddy and a life jacket are your extra lives.
